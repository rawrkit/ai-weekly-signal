## 🏗️ Platforms & infrastructure

**Entire opens its git network preview for coding agents.** Thomas Dohmke's post-GitHub startup — founded February 2026 on a $60M seed at a reported $300M valuation, originally aimed at agent-session memory, review and traceability — has opened a waitlisted preview of a regional GitHub-mirroring network built for AI coding agents. Agents clone and fetch from regional Entire mirrors while GitHub stays upstream. The company claims up to 570K clones/hour, though the benchmark is company-run and the fastest write path depends on Entire-native branches. Native repos, self-hosted nodes and an open-source git backend remain roadmap, not GA. ([WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/07/08/entire-opens-git-network-preview-for-ai-coding-agents-xcxwbn/))

*Why it matters:* the bet is that agents, not humans, become the dominant git client — and that they need different transport economics. Worth watching whether "agent memory and traceability," the original pitch, ships as a product or stays a positioning line.

**Agent infrastructure keeps attracting capital.** July saw ~$1.8B across 12+ AI-agent deals, with enterprise automation taking 58% of it and developer-tooling agents raising $420M (average Series A valuation $185M). Average valuations climbed ~40% QoQ to $280M. Notable rounds: Harvey AI $200M Series C at $2.1B, Lovable $200M Series B at $2.8B, Glean $180M Series D at $2.7B, Hebbia $130M Series B at $1.0B. Bespoke Labs raised a $40M Series A specifically to build simulation environments where agents can be tested and evaluated before production. ([AI Funding](https://aifunding.me/insights/ai-agent-funding-july-2026), [Gravity](https://gravity.fast/blog/ai-agent-funding-tracker-q3-2026/))

*Why it matters:* money is moving from "agent that chats" to "infrastructure that makes agents verifiable" — evaluation, sandboxes, traceability. That is the same gap that keeps biting anyone running agents in production.

## 🧠 Models & capabilities

**Seven releases in seven days.** Between July 17–23, a flagship from Moonshot, three Qwen models, a three-model Gemini drop from Google, an open-weight coding model from poolside, an efficiency MoE from Ant Group, and FLUX 3 from Black Forest Labs. ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/seven-days-seven-releases-july-2026-model-wave))

**Frontier tier moved in a single 24-hour window.** SpaceXAI shipped Grok 4.5, OpenAI shipped GPT-5.6 in three variants (Sol, Terra, Luna), Meta launched Muse Spark 1.1 — triggering an inference price war. GPT-5.6 was reportedly the first frontier model to clear a customer-by-customer US government review before public release. Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24. ([ThursdAI](https://thursdai.news/releases/2026-07), [AIToolsRecap](https://aitoolsrecap.com/Blog/AINewsJuly2026.aspx))

**Open weights got very large.** Moonshot's Kimi K3 landed as the biggest open-weights release to date: 2.8T-parameter sparse MoE, natively multimodal across text/images/video, 1M-token context. Thinking Machines shipped its first model, the 975B Inkling. ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/seven-days-seven-releases-july-2026-model-wave))

*Why it matters:* release cadence has compressed to the point where "latest model" is a weekly variable, not a quarterly one. Two practical consequences: pin model versions in anything you ship, and re-check inference pricing monthly — the price war is real and downward.

### The signal underneath the numbers

Alphabet beat on both revenue and EPS and the stock still fell. The reason is in the same table: free cash flow went negative while capex guidance went up. The market has stopped rewarding AI spending as a growth signal and started pricing it as a cost that has to be justified — cloud revenue acceleration (Google Cloud +82%, margin 35.6%) is now the thing being weighed against the burn.
