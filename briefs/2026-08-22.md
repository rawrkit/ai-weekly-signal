## 🏗️ Platforms & infrastructure

**Stripe buys the router, not the models.** On August 19 Stripe announced an agreement to acquire OpenRouter, the gateway that routes requests across 400+ models from more than 80 providers and picks a destination per request based on task complexity, price, speed and reliability. Stripe named NVIDIA, Zoom and Lovable as OpenRouter customers and disclosed no financial terms; Bloomberg reported the price at more than $7B, against a reported $1.3B valuation earlier this year. Patrick Collison framed the logic in payments terms — "Tokens are the central currency for companies building with AI." ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-agrees-to-acquire-openrouter), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/16/stripe-will-reportedly-acquire-ai-gateway-startup-openrouter-for-7b/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/stripe-openrouter-fintech-ai-model-marketplace-.html))

**Nvidia rents a model factory instead of buying one.** Nvidia agreed to pay Poolside roughly $6B for a non-exclusive license to the system Poolside used to build its models, plus a $1B investment at a $12B pre-money valuation, and extended job offers to 109 Poolside staff. Poolside's investor letter insists this is "not an acquisition and it is not an acquihire" — the three founders stay and the company continues. It is the third time Nvidia has used this license-plus-investment-plus-hiring structure, after Groq and Enfabrica, for a reported ~$27B combined. Separately, Nvidia is reported to be in talks to invest in AI data supplier Mercor at a $20B valuation, double its October 2025 mark; Nvidia is already one of Mercor's largest customers, buying expert-curated data to train its open Nemotron models. Both are reports, not confirmed deals. ([TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-poolside-6bn-model-factory-licence), [Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/sources-poolside-strikes-6-billion), [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/nvidia-discusses-funding-ai-data-supplier-mercor-20-billion-valuation))

*Why it matters:* two very different bets that the money is no longer in the model itself. Stripe is buying the layer that decides which model gets the request — a position that only becomes more valuable as models commoditise and multiply. Nvidia is buying the ability to *make* models and the data to feed them, without taking on a lab. The structure it keeps reusing — license the tech, hire the team, leave the company standing — is worth watching on its own: it moves capability and talent at acquisition scale while sidestepping what an acquisition would trigger.

## 🧠 Models & capabilities

**A lull after the record pace.** Early August set a release record — 11 major models in 20 days by one tracker's count — and this week that stopped. The only notable ship was Z.ai's GLM-5.2 Turbo on August 17, an inference-speed variant of the GLM-5.2 line aimed at long agent execution chains, arriving three days after GLM-5.3. Pricing and benchmark figures circulating for the Turbo variant conflict across trackers and are unverified here. ([LLM Gateway timeline](https://llmgateway.io/timeline), [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/model-updates))

**OpenAI's changes this week were to the rulebook, not the model.** OpenAI published a Model Spec revision dated August 18. It adds a "Be clear about capabilities and limits" section, clarifies how assistants should handle false or unsupported premises, tightens principles for relational interactions with teens — no romantic language, no encouraging emotional dependence, no implying feelings or consciousness — and removes guidance written for pre-reasoning models. OpenAI also added under-18 evaluations to its system cards. ([Model Spec 2026-08-18](https://model-spec.openai.com/2026-08-18.html), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/our-approach-to-the-model-spec/))

*Why it matters:* the quiet week is itself the signal. When releases come 11 in 20 days, independent verification never catches up and the advantage from any single model lasts weeks, not months — so a pause is when the benchmark numbers from the previous fortnight actually get checked. Meanwhile the competitive surface is shifting: OpenAI's shipped artifact this week was a specification of behaviour, which is what you publish when the differentiator is conduct under pressure rather than raw score.

## 🎒 Skills & agent tooling

**MCP publishes its next roadmap.** On August 22 the Model Context Protocol maintainers put out an updated roadmap built on the stateless 2026-07-28 spec, naming five priorities: agentic messaging primitives (server-initiated events via webhooks and channels, composable Tasks, subscriptions, progress notifications); unifying on HTTP-native transport across deployment modes, including local servers running Streamable HTTP over stdio; agent identity and enterprise security via DPoP and Workload Identity Federation so cloud-workload agents authenticate without API keys; standardised tool-result handling and progressive discovery for servers with large tool catalogs; and SDK ergonomics with conformance testing. No dates or version numbers were committed. ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/mcp-roadmap/))

**xAI takes Grok Bot out of beta.** As of August 21, Grok Bot — an agent that runs in the cloud on its own virtual machine, signs into apps, manages files and carries tasks end to end — is included with SuperGrok Plus, Cursor Pro+ and all Cursor Teams plans, ten days after its August 11 beta. Other users get a limited free trial. ([xAI](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/21/grok-bot-is-an-all-new-iphone-and-mac-app-from-spacexai-and-cursor/))

**Skills Radar sat still this week.** The catalog stands at 80 entries with `updated: 2026-08-15` — no commits since the four candidates added that day (knowledge-work-plugins-collection, browser-testing-with-devtools, create-github-issues-for-unmet-specification-requirements, context7-mcp). Status split is 65 in-kit, 11 new, 3 approved, 1 rejected. Install counts are unchanged from the August 8 snapshot: `grill-me` 730.1K, `grilling` 328.8K, `azure-kubernetes` 322.7K, `code-review` 230.2K. The companion [role-kits](https://github.com/rawrkit/role-kits) repo (25 kits) has had no commits since August 8. ([Skills Radar catalog.json](https://github.com/rawrkit/skills-radar/blob/main/catalog.json))

*Why it matters:* the MCP roadmap and the Grok Bot rollout are the same story from two ends. MCP's priority list is dominated by problems that only exist once agents are long-running cloud workloads — how they authenticate without a shared secret, how a server pushes them an event, how they discover tools without loading a thousand definitions. Grok Bot is that architecture shipped to consumers: an agent with its own VM and its own logins. The protocol is racing to specify what products have already started selling.

## 💰 Money

### The week AI stocks broke, and it wasn't about AI

Markets fell on bonds, and AI names took the worst of it. For the week ending August 21 the Nasdaq Composite lost 2.0%, the S&P 500 1.4% and the Dow 0.8%, with the 10-year Treasury yield around 4.73% and the 30-year at 5.27% — the highest for the long bond since 2007. NBC News reported that declines in Microsoft, Nvidia, AMD, Palantir, Oracle and Meta erased more than $820B in market value over the week, with Nvidia down about 7%, Oracle and AMD down about 8.8%, Meta and Microsoft down around 4%, and Super Micro down 23%. Friday brought a partial bounce — Dow +1%, S&P 500 +0.4%, Nasdaq +0.4%. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-august-21-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-bitcoin-080533702.html), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/stock-market-update-ai-losses-rcna242592))

### Chinese AI earnings: the cloud line carries everything

**Alibaba** reported its June quarter on August 18. Total revenue was RMB268,953M, up 9% year over year. AI Cloud and Compute Services revenue reached RMB48,437M ($7,139M), up 45%, with adjusted EBITA for that segment up 133% to RMB5,628M ($830M). AI-related product revenue hit RMB12,376M ($1,824M) — a twelfth consecutive quarter of triple-digit year-over-year growth. Group non-GAAP net income fell 38% to RMB20,715M ($3,053M). ([Alibaba / Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260818525201/en/Alibaba-Group-Announces-June-Quarter-2026-Results))

**Baidu** reported Q2 on August 18 and missed. Total revenue was RMB31.3B ($4.62B), down 4% year over year. Online marketing services fell 19% to RMB13.1B; AI Cloud infrastructure revenue rose 50% to RMB7.3B, with GPU Cloud up 283% year over year (accelerating from 184% in Q1). Net income attributable to Baidu was RMB2.3B ($342M), non-GAAP net income RMB2.6B ($379M), non-GAAP diluted EPS per ADS RMB7.22 ($1.06) — below consensus. Free cash flow was negative RMB7.954B (-$1.173B). No forward guidance was given. Baidu also applied to convert to a dual-primary Hong Kong listing, with a shareholder EGM set for August 26. ([Baidu IR](https://ir.baidu.com/news-releases/news-release-details/baidu-announces-second-quarter-2026-results), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidu-announces-second-quarter-2026-results-302853860.html), [Quartz](https://qz.com/baidu-revenue-miss-advertising-decline-ai-cloud-growth-081826))

### The debt side keeps growing

Bloomberg reported on August 20 that **Broadcom** is in talks with lenders to raise more than $60B for an AI chip financing deal benefiting Anthropic and others — reportedly a ~$30B junior tranche plus a senior-secured tranche of roughly $60–70B that Broadcom would partly guarantee, with Blackstone and Apollo in discussions to participate. It follows the June partnership between Broadcom, Apollo and Blackstone to finance a $35B expansion of Anthropic's compute using Broadcom custom silicon. Earlier in August, **AMD** raised $4.75B in its largest-ever US dollar bond offering across four tranches. Data-center issuers are now paying junk-like yields for investment-grade paper: QTS Realty sold $3.9B of high-grade bonds for a Microsoft-linked Georgia facility at about 7.23%. ([Bloomberg — Broadcom](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/broadcom-seeks-more-than-60-billion-in-latest-ai-debt-deal), [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/broadcom-60bn-ai-chip-debt-anthropic), [Bloomberg — AMD](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/amd-plans-to-raise-as-much-as-5-billion-from-debt-offering), [Bloomberg — AI debt yields](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/juicy-yields-draw-junk-bond-buyers-to-investment-grade-ai-debt))

### Anthropic IPO chatter

CNBC reported on August 21 that Anthropic's IPO filing will list AI backlash as a risk factor; other outlets report a filing as soon as end of August, none of it company-confirmed. What *is* confirmed: Anthropic raised $65B in Series H at a $965B post-money valuation, announced May 28, 2026, led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia. Treat every IPO timing, valuation and underwriter figure circulating this week as unverified reporting. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/21/-anthropic-ipo-filing-will-show-ai-backlash-as-risk-sources-say.html), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h))

*Why it matters:* this week separated AI's operating results from AI's cost of capital, and they moved in opposite directions. Alibaba's cloud segment grew 45% with segment EBITA up 133%, and even Baidu — shrinking overall — grew GPU cloud 283%. Demand is not the problem. The problem is on the financing side: a 30-year yield at a 2007 high, data-center paper clearing above 7% despite investment-grade ratings, and a Broadcom raise structured with a ~$30B junior tranche. The build-out is increasingly funded by debt priced as if it were risky, and the equity market repriced accordingly. Nvidia's August 26 print now lands into a market that has just decided it wants proof.
