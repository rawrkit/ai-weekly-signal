## 🏗️ Platforms & infrastructure

**Agent security becomes its own funding category.** Zenity closed a $125M Series C (backed by SoftBank, Hitachi and LG) to expand its platform for securing autonomous agents running in production at large enterprises. The same week: Hush Security raised a $30M Series A (total funding $41M, with Akamai joining as a strategic investor) to secure the "non-human workforce" of agents and bots; Sapiom raised $35M to help companies control runaway agent costs; and Sail Research raised $80M for long-horizon agent infrastructure. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/03/israeli-startup-zenity-bags-125m-funding-build-security-layer-ai-agents/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/03/softbank-hitachi-lg-back-zenitys-125-million-round-to-police-ai-agents/), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/sapiom-secures-35-million-to-help-companies-control-ai-agent-costs/))

*Why it matters:* last week's money was flowing into "agents that do things"; this week it's flowing into "controlling what agents already do." That's the natural next stage once agents hold real credentials and budgets — governance and cost control stop being nice-to-haves and become a line item.

**Sovereign and specialized agent platforms expand.** NAVER and NVIDIA announced a sovereign AI infrastructure buildout in South Korea, starting with 55 MW of NVIDIA DSX-based capacity at NAVER's GAK Sejong data center (ambition: gigawatt scale), feeding HyperCLOVA X and a Korea-specific AI Agent Platform planned for H2 2026. Separately, Microsoft moved Project Perception — a cybersecurity-focused agent platform for threat detection and response — into public preview on August 3. ([nhimg.org](https://nhimg.org/community/agentic-ai-and-nhis/ai-infrastructure-in-2026-is-control-now-the-real-differentiator/), [aiagentstore.ai](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent-news/this-week))

*Why it matters:* two different flavors of the same trend — AI infrastructure is regionalizing (sovereign compute) and verticalizing (security-specific agent platforms) rather than staying one generic global stack.

## 🧠 Models & capabilities

**Qwen3.8-Max: Alibaba's biggest model yet, and its first Max-class open release.** A 2.4T-parameter MoE (95B active), 1M-token context, natively multimodal (text/image/video). It posts the highest reported PaperBench score (93.0) and leads OSWorld-Verified (86.1, ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol Max at 83.2 and Gemini 3.1 Pro at 76.2), though GPT-5.6 Sol (max) still leads Terminal-Bench 2.1 (88.8 vs Qwen's 86.6). Pricing: $2.00 / $6.00 per 1M input/output tokens. Alibaba says it will open-source a Max-class model for the first time, alongside a smaller Qwen3.8-27B. ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/03/alibaba-qwen-releases-qwen3-8-max/), [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/qwen3-8-max))

**Meta ships a terminal coding agent.** Muse Code (beta, macOS/Linux, one-command install) is powered by Muse Spark 1.2 — 1M-token context, text/image/video/PDF input, trained on long-horizon coding: whole-repository generation, multi-agent coordination, auto-research. It jumps to 82.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (from 76.2% for v1.1) and 59.3% on DeepSWE v1.1 (from 53.0%). Pricing unchanged from v1.1: $1.25 / $4.25 per 1M tokens, cache hits at $0.15. ([Meta AI Research](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-code-and-muse-spark-1-2), [datanorth.ai](https://datanorth.ai/news/meta-releases-muse-spark-1-2-and-muse-code))

**DeepSeek signals the price war has a floor.** DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 (284B, lightweight) entered public beta July 31 at $0.14 / $0.28 per 1M input/output tokens (cache hits as low as $0.0028) — but DeepSeek simultaneously announced a "significant" API price increase is coming, without yet publishing new rates. ([TechBriefly](https://techbriefly.com/2026/08/06/deepseek-api-price-hike-v4-flash/), [pricepertoken.com](https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/deepseek-deepseek-v4-flash))

*Why it matters:* Chinese labs are now setting the pace on agentic benchmarks, not just price — Qwen3.8-Max beats GPT-5.6 and Gemini on two of three cited evals. And DeepSeek raising prices after months of undercutting everyone is the first concrete sign that "inference gets 10x cheaper every year" has a bottom, at least for the labs eating the margin to get there.

## 🎒 Skills & agent tooling

**MCP breaks from its stateful past.** The Model Context Protocol's 2026-07-28 spec release replaces the `initialize`/session-ID handshake with a fully stateless request/response core, adds Multi Round-Trip Requests for server-to-client interactions without holding a stream open, header-based routing so gateways can authorize without parsing JSON bodies, cacheable list responses (`ttlMs`/`cacheScope`), and authorization hardening (RFC 9207 issuer validation, Client ID Metadata Documents replacing Dynamic Client Registration). It's a breaking change — old clients and servers are not compatible with it. Tier-1 SDKs (TypeScript, Python, Go, C#) now see close to half a billion downloads a month combined, with the TS and Python SDKs each past 1 billion total downloads. ([MCP Blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/))

**Skills Radar holds at 68 entries** this week — no net new adds since the last snapshot — still led by decision-quality skills `grill-me` (730K installs) and `grilling` (329K), ahead of `azure-kubernetes` (323K) and engineering skills `code-review` (230K) and `systematic-debugging` (210K). [Role Kits](https://github.com/rawrkit/role-kits) shipped a `role-kit-setup` skill that lets an agent auto-detect a user's role and install the matching kit in one command, rather than the user having to pick from the 23-kit list by hand. ([Skills Radar](https://github.com/rawrkit/skills-radar), [Role Kits](https://github.com/rawrkit/role-kits))

*Why it matters:* MCP moving to a stateless core is the protocol admitting agents run behind the same load balancers and autoscalers as everything else — it's infrastructure now, not a research demo. And the installs data keeps saying the same thing week over week: the most-adopted skills sharpen judgment (grilling a plan, structured decisions), not syntax.

### The pattern holding from last week

Capex is no longer read as a growth signal by default — it's judged case by case against whether revenue backs it up. Microsoft (Azure +43%, crossed $100B annual run rate) and Amazon (AWS +36.7%, 39.4% margin) got rewarded for capex the market can see converting into cloud growth. Meta and Apple got punished — Meta on a profit miss plus a capex raise with no matching upside story, Apple on a Services and China miss unrelated to AI spend. AMD is the odd one out: a genuine beat, but the market wanted proof the Helios AI platform ramp justifies bigger numbers, not just a solid Q3 guide. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/26/big-tech-earnings-meta-microsoft-apple-amazon-market-revolt-ai-spending/))
