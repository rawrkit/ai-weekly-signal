## 🏗️ Platforms & infrastructure

**AIR Security exits stealth with $50M to firewall AI agents.** On September 1, AIR Security — founded in February 2026 by Yair Saban and Niv Hoffman, ~40 staff in Israel — came out of stealth having raised $50M across two rounds in six months: a ~$10M seed led by Sequoia Capital, then a ~$40M round led by Greenoaks, with Swish Ventures and Netz Capital participating. The product is an "inline firewall" that sits in front of an agent and screens the instructions, tools and data entering its context — vetting skills, plugins and MCP servers for injected instructions, excessive permissions and supply-chain risk — before the agent acts, alongside a pre-vetted marketplace of approved add-ons. AIR's launch research claims more than 17,800 public AI add-ons (≈6.7M installations) draw instructions from unverified external sources, that roughly 27% of the add-ons and skills it scanned get filtered out as risky, and that it found live skills impersonating Anthropic and OpenAI capable of executing arbitrary code. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/01/air-security-launches-with-50m-to-build-a-firewall-for-ai-agents/), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-agent-firewall-startup-air-security-emerges-from-stealth-with-50-million/), [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/148163-air-raises-50m-seed-to-build-a-firewall-for-ai-agents/))

**The AI build-out is now visible as contracted backlog, not just capex.** Dell exited its July quarter with a record **$95B AI-server backlog** and **$60.9B of AI-server orders** booked in the quarter; Broadcom's Q4 guide implies **~$21.7B** of AI-semiconductor revenue (+236% YoY), and CEO Hock Tan set multi-year AI-revenue targets of roughly **$115B in fiscal 2027** (up from a prior ">$100B") and **~$230B in fiscal 2028**. Full financial detail is in the Money section. ([Investing.com — Dell slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/dell-q2-fy27-slides-ai-server-backlog-hits-95b-revenue-up-58-93CH-4884718), [Benzinga — Broadcom call transcript](https://www.benzinga.com/news/26/09/61592658/broadcom-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript))

*Why it matters:* two readings of the same boom. The demand side is now legible 12–18 months out because it is contracted — Dell's backlog and Broadcom's multi-year targets are order books, not forecasts, which is why a hardware quarter can move the whole tape. The risk side is the surface all those agents run on: AIR's pitch, and the money behind it, is a bet that what enterprises will pay to secure in 2027 is not the model but the pipe carrying untrusted tools and instructions into it. The Skills section below points the same way.

## 🧠 Models & capabilities

**Five frontier models in about 72 hours — and cyber capability is the new gating axis.** Between September 1 and 3: Anthropic shipped **Claude Fable 5.1** and its restricted-access twin **Mythos 5.1**, claiming records on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and Humanity's Last Exam, cutting Fable cache-read pricing to ~25% of the prior rate, and flagging breaking changes for Fable 5 integrations (new refusal handling, model-fallback options, new billing rules); Mythos 5.1 is limited to registered Anthropic partners in cybersecurity or life sciences ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/anthropics-new-fable-release-is-cheaper-less-restrictive/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropics-claude-fable-5-1-and-mythos-5-1-arrive-with-a-75-cost-reduction-for-fable-cache-reads)). **OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra** (Sep 3) is the first model to cross OpenAI's "critical cybersecurity capability" threshold — the public version restricts advanced cyber tasks, with full capability limited to its Daybreak partner program; OpenAI reported 100% on ExploitBench and 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 with tools (vendor-reported), and Greg Brockman said "it's not unreasonable to feel that we are now in the AGI era" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/03/openai-debuts-gpt-6-astra-computer-use-greg-brockman-says-start-of-agi/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/open-ai-astra-gpt-6-cyber.html)). **Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash** (Sep 2) shipped with a defenders-only "3.8 Flash Cyber" variant gated by its Fairwind program, with a claimed >70% real-world vulnerability-discovery rate ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gemini-3-8-flash-cyber), [eesel AI](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-3-8-flash)). **Meta's Muse Spark 1.3** (Sep 2) and Alibaba's **Qwen3.8-Max-0902** snapshot rounded out the wave, both aimed at longer-horizon coding and agentic work ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/muse-spark-1-3), [TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/09/02/alibaba-upgrades-qwen38-max-with-new-0902-snapshot/)).

**"The price" of a model is now a schedule, not a number.** Gemini 3.8 Flash launched at **$0.75 / $3.75** per million input / output tokens — but only through December 31, 2026, with both figures scheduled to double to $1.50 / $7.50 on January 1 ([codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/gemini-3-8-flash-complete-guide-2026/), [eesel AI](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-3-8-flash)). Fable 5.1 held its list price ($10 / $50 per million) while cutting cache reads ~75% ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropics-claude-fable-5-1-and-mythos-5-1-arrive-with-a-75-cost-reduction-for-fable-cache-reads)). Qwen3.8-Max-0902 kept its $2 / $6 pricing flat while claiming gains across all eight coding benchmarks in its scorecard, including Code Arena WebDev where its vendor numbers put it just ahead of Claude Opus 5 Max — vendor-reported, not independently verified ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/qwen3-8-max), [CellCog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/qwen3-8-max-0902/)).

*Why it matters:* two structural shifts under the release noise. First, the frontier labs have converged on the same containment design — ship a public model with cyber capability dialled down, plus a full-strength sibling behind a vetted-partner program (Daybreak, Fairwind, Mythos). Cyber capability, not raw benchmark score, is now the axis that decides who gets access. Second, the gains this wave came from post-training and environment scaling rather than new base architectures — which is also why pricing has turned into a moving target of promos, cancellations and scheduled step-ups that anyone budgeting for inference now has to track like a contract.

## 🎒 Skills & agent tooling

**Skills Radar adds five entries, all code-quality behaviour.** The catalog moved to **92 entries** (from 87) with `updated: 2026-08-30`. All five additions modify how an agent works rather than adding domain knowledge: `spec-driven-development` (29.3K installs), `code-review-and-quality` (33.7K), `documentation-and-adrs` (28.0K), `thermo-nuclear-code-quality-review` (13.4K) and `what-context-needed` (8.9K). Status split is 65 in-kit / 23 new / 3 approved / 1 rejected. The install leaderboard is unchanged from last week — `improve-codebase-architecture` 775.8K, `grill-me` 730.1K, `handoff` 654.0K, `diagnosing-bugs` 456.7K, `brainstorming` 336.6K, `grilling` 328.8K, `azure-kubernetes` 322.7K, `code-review` 230.2K — with the five new entries arriving in "new" status on small install bases. The [role-kits](https://github.com/rawrkit/role-kits) repo (23 kits) still has no commits since August 8. ([Skills Radar catalog.json](https://github.com/rawrkit/skills-radar/blob/main/catalog.json))

**A security firm says a meaningful slice of the skill supply is unsafe.** AIR Security's launch research (see Platforms) reports 17,800+ public AI add-ons drawing instructions from unverified sources, ~27% of the skills and add-ons it scanned filtered out as risky, and live skills impersonating Anthropic and OpenAI that can run arbitrary code. Vendor-reported, tied to a product launch, and not independently verified — but directionally consistent with the catalog's own governance model of vetting before adoption. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/01/air-security-launches-with-50m-to-build-a-firewall-for-ai-agents/))

*Why it matters:* the catalog trend holds for a second straight week — every new entry is a workflow-behaviour skill (review gates, spec-first, context checks), none is a knowledge pack. The bottleneck for agents in real work keeps looking like discipline, not information. AIR's numbers are the counterweight from the security side: as installing skills becomes the default way to extend an agent, the marketplace becomes an attack surface, and "add a skill" and "screen every skill before it reaches the context" are turning into the same conversation.

## 💰 Money

Broadcom and Dell both reported into September — historically the market's worst month — and both cleared expectations on the AI line. The tape still split them.

### Broadcom: a $16.7B AI quarter and a $230B FY2028 target

Broadcom reported Q3 FY2026 revenue of **$29.6B, up 86%** year over year, ahead of the ~$29.4B consensus. Semiconductor solutions were $20.8B (+127%); infrastructure software $8.8B (+29%). **AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7B, up 221% year over year and 54% sequentially** — 56% of total revenue. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was **$3.32** (vs ~$3.24 expected); GAAP EPS was $2.68. Non-GAAP gross margin was ~75% and non-GAAP operating margin ~68%. **Free cash flow was $13.7B, 46% of revenue**; operating cash flow was $14.2B; the quarterly dividend is $0.65. Q4 FY2026 revenue is guided to **~$34.8B (+93%)**, with AI-semiconductor revenue of **~$21.7B (+236%)**. On the call, Hock Tan raised the multi-year framing to roughly **$115B of AI revenue in fiscal 2027** (from a prior ">$100B") and **~$230B in fiscal 2028**. The stock fell about 3% after hours as the Q4 total-revenue guide landed just shy of the most bullish expectations despite the long-term raise. ([PRNewswire / Broadcom](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/broadcom-inc-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results-and-quarterly-dividend-302868129.html), [StockTitan — AVGO 8-K](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/AVGO/8-k-broadcom-inc-reports-material-event-f19c72b0f907.html), [Benzinga — call transcript](https://www.benzinga.com/news/26/09/61592658/broadcom-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript))

### Dell: $47B in revenue, a $95B AI backlog, a ~$25B guide raise

Dell reported Q2 FY2027 revenue of **$47.0B, up 58%** year over year. Infrastructure Solutions Group was **$31.8B (+89%)**; within it, **AI-optimised server revenue was $16.4B (+100%)**, with **$60.9B of AI-server orders** booked in the quarter and backlog rising to a record **$95B**. Traditional servers and networking were $10.5B (+122%), storage $4.9B (+26%), and Client Solutions Group $15.0B (+20%). GAAP diluted EPS was **$6.34 (+273%)** and non-GAAP EPS **$7.04 (+203%)**; cash flow from operations was $2.2B. Dell raised full-year FY2027 revenue guidance to **$192.0B (+69%)** — about $25B above the prior ~$167B — with non-GAAP EPS guidance of **$25.50**. Shares rose after the print, reported between ~9% and ~16% depending on the trading session referenced. ([SEC 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001571996/000157199626000039/exhibit991earnings8kq2fy27.htm), [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/dell-q2-fy27-slides-ai-server-backlog-hits-95b-revenue-up-58-93CH-4884718), [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/02/dell-surges-9-on-record-95b-ai-backlog-hewlett-packard-enterprise-climbs-4-super-micro-ticks-up/), [Yahoo Finance](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/dell-stock-jumps-nearly-10-155208752.html))

### Anthropic's private mark — still the swing factor, no confirmed move this week

Nothing this week confirmed a change to Anthropic's IPO timeline. Reporting continues to point to a possible Nasdaq listing as early as October 2026, against a revenue run-rate reported near **$65B** at end-July and a **$965B** Series H mark, with secondary-market implied valuations circulating around **$1.05–1.15T**. All of that is reporting, not company-confirmed, and every valuation and date figure should be treated as such. It stays on the watchlist because, as the last brief laid out, its paper value is now the largest single swing factor across several public AI companies' balance sheets. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-confidentially-files-ipo-965-billion-valuation/), [GraniteShares](https://graniteshares.com/research/anthropic-ipo-2026-explained-from-965-billion-to-a-possible-2-trillion-listing/))

*Why it matters:* the hardware prints answered the "is the AI trade breaking in September" question the way Nvidia did in August — bigger numbers, longer visibility — but the reactions show a market grading to near-perfection. Dell rallied on a backlog it cannot fill for a year; Broadcom sold off despite doubling AI revenue and naming a $230B target, because one quarter's total guide came in a hair light. The order book is now treated as the floor, and anything short of beat-and-raise on every line reads as a miss. The cash is real — Broadcom's $13.7B of quarterly free cash flow is not a mark-to-market gain — but the multiples now assume that backlog converts on schedule.
