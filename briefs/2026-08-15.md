## 🏗️ Platforms & infrastructure

**Washington starts mapping how China reaches Nvidia chips it isn't allowed to buy.** The Bureau of Industry and Security is reviewing how Chinese AI labs access advanced Nvidia hardware through offshore rentals — non-Chinese data centers in third countries like Singapore and Malaysia obtain US chips license-free, then lease compute to Chinese customers such as ByteDance and Alibaba. The trigger was a run of results that made China's access to frontier-class hardware hard to deny despite the export regime: Qwen3.8-Max reportedly ran autonomously on a software project for 16 straight days, and DeepSeek's V4 Flash lands within two points of Claude Opus 4.7 on SWE-bench Verified at roughly 1% of the cost. For now this is a mapping exercise, not enforcement — under EAR Section 734.2, remote cloud access doesn't meet the legal definition of "export," so BIS currently lacks statutory authority to stop it. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/us-reviews-china-s-offshore-access-to-nvidia-chips-after-ai-breakthroughs), [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/us-bis-review-china-remote-offshore-nvidia-chip-access))

**Salesforce widens the agent-data perimeter instead of trying to own it.** On August 11, Salesforce expanded "zero-copy" links between Agentforce and Databricks, Google Cloud, Snowflake and AWS — letting agents query data in place across those platforms rather than requiring customers to migrate it into Salesforce's own stack. The company said Agentforce annual recurring revenue hit $1.2B in its most recent quarter, more than tripling year over year. ([Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/91829/salesforce-expands-zero-copy-ai-agent-ecosystem-with-google-and-databricks-links), [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/salesforce-databricks-shared-foundation-of-human-agent-work-announcement/))

*Why it matters:* export control is a legal category, and BIS is discovering that "cloud rental" doesn't fit inside it — the enforcement gap it's now trying to map is the same one Chinese labs already used to keep pace on frontier benchmarks. Salesforce's move is the enterprise mirror image: no single vendor is winning the fight to be the one place agent data lives, so the money is shifting into being the best-connected node instead.

## 🧠 Models & capabilities

**GLM-5.3 gets so good at offensive cyber tasks that Z.ai holds back its own weights.** Released August 14, GLM-5.3 runs on the same 743B-parameter base as GLM-5.2 — every reported gain comes from roughly two months of additional post-training and reinforcement learning on harder executable environments. By Z.ai's own figures it now leads CyberGym at 84.5%, ahead of Claude Mythos 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol, and its agentic terminal-coding score jumped from 4.6 to 28.3. The model is live now via API and the Coding Plan ($18/mo Lite, $80 Pro, $168 Max), but Z.ai is withholding the open weights that made the GLM line matter for about two weeks, pending a safety review — the cyber capability reportedly grew faster than the company expected as training scaled. ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/14/z-ai-ships-glm-5-3-without-retraining-the-base-model-better-at-complex-coding-and-long-horizon-tasks/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/14/china-open-source-ai-glm-53))

**Google ships the workhorse while the flagship stays delayed.** Gemini 3.7 Flash launched August 13 for coding and agent workloads, three weeks after its predecessor, at an introductory price of $0.75/$3.75 per 1M input/output tokens (rising to $1.50/$7.50 on January 1, 2027) — half the launch price of Gemini 3.6 Flash. Google says it beats comparable Anthropic and OpenAI models across nine benchmarks, including a jump from 49.0% to 65.3% on DeepSWE v1.1 and a Code Arena Elo of 1588 versus 1541 for Claude Sonnet 5 and 1523 for GPT-5.6 Terra. Gemini 3.5 Pro, the actual flagship, remains delayed. ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/google-debuts-new-gemini-flash-while-top-ai-model-still-delayed))

*Why it matters:* two labs, two different reasons for holding back the top of the line — Z.ai citing a safety review it didn't expect to need, Google citing a delay it hasn't explained — while both ship a cheaper, coding-and-agent-tuned tier in the meantime. Benchmarks are increasingly reported on agentic coding tasks specifically, not general knowledge, because that's what labs are now competing to sell.

## 🎒 Skills & agent tooling

**Claude Code stops asking permission by default.** Starting August 14, auto mode is the default permission mode for new Claude Code sessions on Pro, Max and Team plans (Enterprise, API and cloud-platform users follow within the month). A classifier now routes tool calls automatically, blocking anything it judges irreversible, destructive or aimed outside your environment; explicit deny/ask rules are still evaluated first and still take precedence. Existing users get a one-time prompt to opt in or out on their next session. ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4207959/anthropic-makes-claude-codes-auto-mode-default-for-paid-users.html), [Claude Code Docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/auto-mode-config))

**Skills Radar grows to 76 entries and adds a validator.** The catalog is up from 68 entries a week ago after last week's batch of seven candidates was joined by `read-the-room` on August 9. The same day, the maintainers shipped `validate.py` plus CI to catch catalog problems before they ship — required fields, known statuses/roles, duplicate ids, and a UTF-8 BOM that had been silently breaking strict JSON parsers for any agent following the catalog's own documented integration path. Installs data in the catalog is still the August 8 snapshot (`grill-me` 730.1K, `grilling` 328.8K, `azure-kubernetes` 322.7K) — no refresh yet this week. ([Skills Radar](https://github.com/rawrkit/skills-radar))

*Why it matters:* removing the per-action prompt is the biggest change to how agentic coding tools actually feel to use since they were introduced — the classifier, not the user, becomes the first line of judgment on most actions. And a catalog that only now added format validation is a small tell about where the ecosystem actually is: past the stage of hand-checking every entry, not yet at the stage of taking that infrastructure for granted.

## 💰 Money

### A quiet earnings week — the story is export policy, not results

None of the core cohort reported this week: Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, Apple, AMD and Palantir all reported in the dense stretch covered in last week's brief, and Alphabet reported back on July 22 (Google Cloud revenue +82% YoY to $24.8B, FY26 capex guide raised to $195–205B) — already old news by this brief's window. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/alphabet-q2-2026-earnings-revenue-203058727.html))

The financially relevant development this week is the one covered in Platforms above: BIS's review of how Chinese firms rent Nvidia compute offshore. It carries direct earnings-season stakes — Nvidia reports August 26, and the review is a response to exactly the kind of Chinese-lab capability jump (Qwen3.8-Max, DeepSeek V4 Flash) that raises questions about how much control Nvidia's own export compliance actually has over where its chips' compute ends up.

**Private funding stayed at normal (non-mega) size this week.** Singapore's Graas raised a $17M Series B led by Temasek's LemmaTree and acquired product-data startup Trustana, folding its data into Graas's "Commerce Knowledge Graph" for retail agents — a reminder that outside the handful of AI mega-rounds, the more typical agentic-AI raise this year is still tens of millions, not billions. ([TechNode Global](https://technode.global/2026/08/13/singapores-graas-raises-17-million-series-b-and-acquires-product-data-startup-trustana/))
