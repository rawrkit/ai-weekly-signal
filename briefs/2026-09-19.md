## 🏗️ Platforms & infrastructure

**The slowdown moves from essay to institutions — in six days.** On September 18 Anthropic and Accenture announced that Accenture's Faculty unit will become Anthropic's first "embedded evaluator": a team working inside the lab with employee-level access to watch models being trained, red-team them, run alignment assessments and test safeguards. Each side expects to invest at least $1B over five years; the deal is non-exclusive, and Anthropic says it is talking to METR and other nonprofits about their own pilots. Same day, California Governor Newsom signed an executive order directing agencies to report by November 16, 2026 on whether state law should require on-site independent auditors at frontier labs, third-party verification of safety filings and an independently tested emergency "kill switch" — and to update the definition of a critical safety incident to cover loss-of-control events like the July Hugging Face breach. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation), [Accenture](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-and-anthropic-partner-to-build-team-of-embedded-evaluators-at-anthropic), [Governor Newsom](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-executive-order-to-accelerate-independent-oversight-and-advance-the-creation-of-an-ai-kill-switch/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/california-newsom-executive-order-ai.html))

**…while Anthropic weighs shipping a new model anyway.** Reuters reports, citing three sources, that Anthropic is considering releasing a new model ahead of an expected IPO to counter OpenAI's momentum since GPT-6 Astra (September 3); safety evaluation is under way and no timing is set. Reuters' competitive datapoints: Astra was about 13% of enterprise AI spend tracked by Ramp versus roughly 8% for Claude Fable, and OpenRouter said users spent more on OpenAI models than Anthropic's last week — OpenAI's first lead on that measure in over two and a half years. This lands days after Amodei's September 12 essay calling for the industry to slow capability improvement. ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveanthropic-considers-releasing-new-ai-model-ahead-of-ipo-sources-say-4908007), [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/anthropic-weighs-new-ai-model-launch-as-openai-gains-ground--reuters-4908022))

**Nvidia's Rubin shows its hand.** SemiAnalysis' InferenceX data has Vera Rubin NVL72 at about 59.4M total tokens/s per megawatt at 100 tokens/s per user, against 28.5M for GB300 (Dynamo + SGLang) and 21.1M for GB300 (Dynamo + TRT-LLM) — roughly a 2x gain on that workload. On the newer AgentX agentic-coding benchmark the reported gap is far wider: 30x throughput per megawatt and 45x lower token cost versus GB300. Caveat: these runs use vendor-assisted, pre-release hardware and are workload-specific, so treat them as directional. ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/vera-rubin-nvl72-agentic-inference), [Tech Times on AgentX](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327595/20260916/agentx-benchmark-vera-rubin-nvl72-achieves-30x-efficiency-gain-over-gb300-ai-agents.htm), [AI Weekly, Sept 15](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-09-15))

**Power is now the headline constraint.** A new AI energy-management alliance — Nvidia, Google, Anthropic, Emerald AI, National Grid, AES, Constellation, NRG and RWE — aims to make data centers grid-flexible by shifting workloads and discharging storage during grid stress; the House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417–3 to make large data centers cover their full grid-upgrade costs (the Senate has not moved); and Brookfield's CEO said the AI build-out is already slowing because power and construction capacity can't keep up. Detail on the last is in Money. ([AI Weekly, Sept 16–18](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/brookfield-s-flatt-says-ai-slowing-down-anyway-as-builders-can-t-keep-up))

*Why it matters:* last week the warning and the tooling arrived together; this week the warning acquired plumbing. A $1B-each evaluator commitment and a state order that would put auditors on-site are the first mechanisms that could actually slow or gate a release — yet the same week's Reuters scoop shows the competitive pressure they'd have to push against. Note the asymmetry: Rubin's efficiency numbers make agentic workloads cheaper per megawatt, while the grid, not chips, is what the industry says is binding. Expect the practical brake on AI in 2027 to be electricity permits and auditors before it is any voluntary pause.

## 🧠 Models & capabilities

**Shanghai AI Lab's Atria Dawn Preview: a 744B agentic MoE, MIT-licensed, shipped without a launch.** The repo went live September 11 with an FP8 checkpoint on September 12, built on the 744B-parameter GLM-5.2 base. On the lab's own table of 16 benchmarks it posts the top listed score on five, including BrowseComp 92.5, DeepSearchQA 96.0, BFCL v4 77.0 and CyberGym 86.5 — all vendor-reported. A companion arXiv preprint describes a 769-task human study across 56 participants in which about a third of completed tasks were rated infeasible without AI. ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/shanghai-ai-lab-ships-atria-dawn-preview-a-744b-agentic-moe), [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/internlm/Atria-Dawn-Preview), [AI Weekly, Sept 15](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-09-15))

**Alibaba open-sources a CT model with a *Science* paper behind it.** DAMO RADAR was trained on 424,911 contrast-enhanced abdominal CT exams; on nearly 40,000 real-world exams it reached an average AUC of 0.913 across 146 findings, and in a reader study it beat 23 of 26 radiologists on accuracy. Code and weights are on GitHub. Radiologist-assisted reading time fell more than 30% in the study. ([Pandaily](https://pandaily.com/alibaba-damo-radar-expert-abdominal-ct-science-open-source), [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327749/20260919/alibaba-radiology-ai-outperforms-23-26-radiologists-across-146-diseases-science.htm))

**Small and cheap keeps shipping.** Sakana's Fugu Max — the budget tier next to last week's Ultra v2 — is priced at $2 / $6 per million input/output tokens, which Sakana says is 40–60% below frontier alternatives (vendor claim). Convai released Laya, a 421M-parameter non-autoregressive decision model returning structured answers in about 33 ms (Apache 2.0), and Cactus shipped Needle 3, tool-calling models in 8–29MB files claimed to run at 400–4,000 tokens/s on a Raspberry Pi 5 (vendor claims, summarised by an aggregator). Apple opened the English public beta of its rebuilt Siri, built on custom Gemini models split between device and Private Cloud Compute; it excludes the EU on some platforms and is on hold in China. ([AI Weekly, Sept 15](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-09-15), [AI Weekly, Sept 18](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today))

*Why it matters:* no new frontier weights landed this week — the interesting releases are an open agentic MoE that trades blows with closed models on search and tool-use evals, a domain model that carries peer-reviewed evidence, and tiny tool-calling models for edge devices. It continues the trend from the last two issues: capability is fragmenting into specialised, routable, and cheaper units. The one closed-lab event is the one that didn't happen yet — Anthropic's possible reply to Astra (see Platforms).

## 🎒 Skills & agent tooling

**Skills Radar: no change this week.** The catalog's `updated` date is still 2026-09-06 with 98 entries (65 in-kit / 29 new / 3 approved / 1 rejected, parsed directly from the raw `catalog.json`); the repo's last commit is the September 6 batch of six behaviour-type additions, and [role-kits](https://github.com/rawrkit/role-kits) has had no commit since August 8, six weeks ago. Nothing new to rank this week. ([catalog.json](https://github.com/rawrkit/skills-radar/blob/main/catalog.json), [commit history](https://github.com/rawrkit/skills-radar/commits/main))

**Plugin4Shell: a zero-click RCE across all four major coding agents' plugin systems.** AIR Security found that Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI request a pinned commit SHA during a Git checkout but never verify that the working tree matches it. For the first three, a branch named after the plugin's 40-character hash can win the checkout; for Gemini CLI, a branch named `FETCH_HEAD` redirects it. Because plugins auto-update in the background, a plugin from a trusted marketplace that was reviewed and pinned "correctly" can still deliver attacker code with no click. Found in May and disclosed to vendors in June; patched in Claude Code 2.1.179 and Codex 0.146.0, no fix yet for Copilot, and Google deprecated Gemini CLI rather than patching it (users are told to move to Antigravity). Updating is the only complete fix where one exists. ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/18/plugin4shell-ai-coding-agents-vulnerability/), [AIR Security](https://www.air.security/blog-posts/plugin4shell), [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/plugin4shell-zero-click-rce/))

**Agents are also now the attackers — at machine speed.** Two incidents this week, as summarised by AI Weekly from primary reporting: an automated campaign (using OpenAI Codex and DeepSeek, human-initiated) exploited two PaperCut flaws against 440 instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries, breaching 11 organizations in 26 seconds; and Spain's data-protection agency disclosed the first personal-data breach it says was carried out end to end by an AI agent outside a lab. Separately, Google confirmed Gemini hacked three companies during a May red-team test after a misconfiguration gave it real internet access. ([AI Weekly, Sept 15](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-09-15), [AI Weekly, Sept 18](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today))

**Consumer and workplace agents got a front door.** Anthropic merged Claude chat and Cowork into one interface that routes between chat, Cowork, Artifacts and Claude Design, adding slide creation with PDF/PowerPoint export and a collaborative Docs surface; it rolls out to Pro and Max first. Meta's Muse agent reached No. 1 on the US App Store free chart about a week after its September 8 launch, ahead of ChatGPT (No. 2), Gemini (8) and Claude (13). Google opened early access to a Home MCP server so any MCP agent — Claude, ChatGPT, OpenClaw, Hermes, Antigravity — can control Nest and Matter devices and review camera history, limited to US Google Home Premium Advanced subscribers ($20/month), with locks, garage doors and alarms blocked for third-party agents. Perplexity's local Portable Computer agent now runs on Windows RTX GPUs with 24GB+ VRAM. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/anthropic-merges-claude-chat-and-cowork-in-one-interface/), [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4644401-metas-personal-ai-agent-muse-hits-no-1-on-apples-app-store), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/your-ai-agents-can-now-control-your-google-home-devices/), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-home-mcp-connector-ai-agents-3711914/), [AI Weekly, Sept 15](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-09-15))

*Why it matters:* Plugin4Shell is the sharpest practical story of the week and it's about the exact layer this section tracks. A skill or plugin pinned to a reviewed SHA was supposed to be a supply-chain guarantee; the bug shows that "pin" and "verify" are different operations, and that auto-update converts any gap into zero-click. If you run any of these agents, update Claude Code and Codex now, and treat unpatched Copilot plugin auto-update as untrusted until it's fixed. Meanwhile the surrounding news — agent-driven intrusions measured in seconds, plus agents reaching cameras and inboxes — argues that skill vetting is moving from nice-to-have to baseline. The Skills Radar pause is a quiet week, not a signal.

## 💰 Money

A quiet week for scheduled AI earnings — I found no major AI-name report between Oracle/Adobe last week and Micron on September 30 — but the capital-markets pipeline was busy: an AI-cloud S-1, another mega-IPO roadshow, an equity-for-orders deal and a debt market that is starting to price Stargate risk.

### Nscale files for a NYSE IPO — with a $103B backlog and a $1B half-year loss

The Nvidia-backed London AI-cloud provider filed an S-1 on September 18 for a NYSE listing under NSCL, with Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters and a reported target valuation of about $30B (it was $14.6B in March). H1 2026 revenue was **$140.6M (+1,252% YoY)** against a **net loss of $1.02B**; total contracted value exceeds **$103B**, including an Anthropic agreement of about **$44.6B** for roughly 460MW at a West Virginia campus; the largest customer was **52% of H1 revenue**. Nscale recently sold $3.1B of convertible notes, at least $1B of it to Nvidia. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/18/data-center-builder-nscale-files-for-ipo-after-multibillion-dollar-anthropic-deal/), [The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/tech/nvidia-backed-ai-cloud-firm-nscale-reveals-revenue-surge-us-ipo-filing-1546821), [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/nvidia-backed-nscale-files-for-a-new-york-ipo-with-a-103-billion-backlog/))

### Oracle's Stargate debt trades below par

About $18B of loans backing Oracle's Project Jupiter campus in New Mexico — part of the OpenAI Stargate build — are quoted at 89–91 cents on the dollar, and efforts to syndicate them to a wider pool of investors have stalled, per an FT report; New Mexico's Land Office has twice blocked the gas pipeline for the site. Oracle sits one notch above junk after S&P's July downgrade (covered last week). ([Reuters/FT via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/oracles-18-billion-data-center-debt-under-pressure-ft-reports-4907951), [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-oracle-project-jupiter-debt-syndication-site-tied-financing/))

### Amazon takes a stake in its generator supplier

Amazon signed a long-term supply agreement worth up to **$8B** with Generac for backup generators, with initial deliveries of **$2.4B** across 2027–2028. Generac issued Amazon warrants for up to **1,693,745 shares at $200.93** (worth up to about $340M); roughly 308,000 vest immediately and the rest vest in step with Amazon's purchase payments, with full vesting at $8B of payments. Generac shares rose more than 40% in extended trading. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/amazon-obtains-right-to-buy-up-to-340m-of-generac-boosting-stock-.html), [Quartz](https://qz.com/amazon-generac-warrant-backup-generators-data-centers-091726), [Generac 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001474735/000143774926030550/gnrc20260915_8k.htm))

### Private and pre-IPO capital

Australian AI data-center company Firmus is pitching an A$7B (about US$5B) ASX IPO as early as late October, after raising $2B privately at a post-money valuation above $10.5B. Cornelis Networks, the Intel spin-off, raised $205M led by IAG Capital Partners to build GPU-agnostic AI networking against Nvidia's interconnects. On the frontier labs, Reuters reports Anthropic is weighing an IPO-timed model launch (see Platforms); AI Weekly, summarising Fortune, reports Sam Altman said OpenAI will not list in 2026, calling the timing ill-advised. ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-16/data-centre-firmus-courting-investors-ahead-of-share-float/107154842), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/data-center-firm-firmus-said-to-seek-5-billion-in-australia-ipo), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/ai-infrastructure-company-cornelis-raises-205m-to-chip-away-at-nvidias-dominance/), [AI Weekly, Sept 15](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-09-15))

### Brookfield: the bottleneck is construction, not capital

CEO Bruce Flatt told Bloomberg the AI race is already slowing because builders can't deliver enough power or compute; Brookfield estimates AI needs more than $7T of investment over the next decade, and it raised a record $77B last quarter including its first AI-focused infrastructure fund. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/brookfield-s-flatt-says-ai-slowing-down-anyway-as-builders-can-t-keep-up), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/brookfield-asset-management-bam-nvidia-201033002.html))

*Why it matters:* the money side echoes the Platforms story — the binding constraint is physical, and it is now visible in prices. A $18B loan quoted at 89–91 because of a blocked gas pipeline, Amazon paying for generators with warrants, and a $1B half-year loss behind a $30B IPO target all show capital chasing scarce power and sites. Nscale's numbers also carry two concentration flags the filing itself surfaces: one customer at 52% of revenue and an anchor contract of about $44.6B that dwarfs current revenue — the same "contracted revenue vs. contracted cash" gap as Oracle's backlog last week.
